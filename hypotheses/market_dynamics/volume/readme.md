### Summary
- **Holds (strengthened by run 2):**
  - Volume and volatility move together, and lower-than-usual volume means lower volatility even after the market-wide trend is removed (V-11/12, RV-05/06/09, FU-V13).
  - Volume is seasonal (VT-01/02/04/05).
  - Volume has strong long memory: d ≈ 0.5, so its deviations persist rather than overshoot (V-03, V-04, RV-07).
  - Deviations still decay toward a *local* centre within days (V-02).
  - High-volume spells can be anticipated, and their end forecast, better than seasonal baselines (V-05, V-06).
  - High-volume price areas get revisited, on 1-day and 30-day profiles (FU-VP01, VP-14).
  - Low-volume drops and jumps are recovered more, at the same jump size (VP-02, FU-VP04), and low-volume gaps fill more (VP-13).
  - Very low A/D leads to a bounce (VA-07).
- **Weakened or dropped by run 2:**
  - The POC magnet does not hold when conditioned on volume known in advance (FU-VP12).
  - The flow-disagreement edge is far below trading costs (FU-VP22).
  - Breakout volume does not help, and at 30 days it hurts (FU-VA01).
  - Only the middle dip of the down-trend volume profile holds up (FU-VTR02).
- **Opposite of the expectation:**
  - Short-horizon volume × return and taker-flow signals predict **reversal** (VP-16–21).
  - Low-volume periods are, if anything, *up* days (V-10, V-16).
  - Volume spikes are followed by more high volume, not less (RV-07, V-04, V-05).
- **Volume adds nothing over price:** forecasting realised volatility (RV-03/04), the action principle (VP-19) and the "settled level" pattern (VA-08).


# Volume distribution, memory and duration

[Category index](../START_HERE.md) · [Source guide](../source_index/Sources.md)

**How does participation evolve on its own?** Distribution, moving baseline, persistence, overshoot and lengths of spells.

The earlier register described volume as both mean reverting and persistent. **Run 2 reconciles the two.** Deviations from a *local* centre decay within days (V-02). But the process has strong long memory (d ≈ 0.5), so deviations persist rather than overshoot to the other side (V-04, RV-07). The centre itself drifts: dollar volume falls on 8/9 assets, and coin volume falls faster (VT-06). (LOG/V-02, LOG/V-27.)

**Test design.** Base-asset quantity, quote notional and trade count are kept separate. Genuine zero-volume bars (median 0.01% of minutes) are counted separately from outages. Seasonal medians, trends and thresholds are fitted on earlier observations only. Models are fitted on the first 70% of each asset and scored on the last 30%.
